Glossary

Every term the games use, in plain words, for the Indian market (NSE, NIFTY 50). Tap a word in a game to land here.

Basics Price Greeks Option chain Selling Costs Strategies

Basics

Option
A contract that gives you the right, not the obligation, to a payout based on where NIFTY ends on expiry day. You pay a price (the premium) for that right.
CE (call option)
Pays ₹65 (one lot) for every point NIFTY ends above the strike. Buy a CE when you expect NIFTY to rise.
PE (put option)
Pays ₹65 for every point NIFTY ends below the strike. Buy a PE when you expect NIFTY to fall.
Strike
The NIFTY level written into the contract, in steps of 50 (23,950, 24,000, 24,050 …). "24000 CE" is a call with strike 24,000.
Lot
You can't buy one option; you buy a lot. A NIFTY lot is 65 units (Jan 2026 series), so a premium of ₹120 costs ₹120 × 65 = ₹7,800. BANK NIFTY's lot is 30.
Expiry
The day the contract is settled. NIFTY weekly options expire every Tuesday at 15:30; monthly contracts on the last Tuesday. BANK NIFTY has monthly expiry only.
Cash settlement
Index options never deliver shares. At 15:30 on expiry day an in-the-money option is settled in cash for its intrinsic value; an out-of-the-money one expires worth nothing.
ATM / ITM / OTM
At the money: strike closest to NIFTY. In the money: a CE below NIFTY or a PE above it, so it already has value. Out of the money: the opposite; it only pays if NIFTY moves past the strike.
0DTE
"Zero days to expiry": an option traded on its expiry day. Its time value goes to zero by 15:30, which is why cheap 0DTE options usually end worthless.

Price

Premium
The option's price per unit, in NIFTY points. ₹ for a lot = premium × 65.
Intrinsic value
What the option would pay if settled right now: for a CE, NIFTY minus strike if positive; for a PE, strike minus NIFTY if positive; otherwise zero.
Time value
Premium minus intrinsic value: what you pay for the chance that NIFTY moves in your favour before expiry. It melts to zero by expiry.
Implied volatility (IV)
How big a move the option's price assumes, as a yearly percentage. Higher IV = dearer options. IV rises before events (RBI policy, Budget, results) and drops after: the "IV crush".
India VIX
NSE's index of the IV of near-term NIFTY options. 12–15 is calm; 20+ is nervous; it jumps when NIFTY falls.
Skew
Out-of-the-money puts carry a higher IV than out-of-the-money calls, because crashes are faster than rallies and people pay up for protection.
Bid / ask / LTP
Bid: the best price someone will pay you. Ask: the best price someone will sell at. You buy at the ask and sell at the bid; the gap is the spread, a cost. LTP: last traded price.

Greeks

Delta
How much the premium changes when NIFTY moves 1 point. An ATM option has a delta near 0.5 (₹32 per point per lot). |Delta| is also a rough probability of expiring in the money.
Theta
How much the premium loses per day if nothing moves. Buyers pay theta; sellers earn it. It speeds up as expiry nears.
Vega
How much the premium changes when IV moves 1 point. Buyers gain when IV rises and lose in an IV crush.
Gamma
How fast delta itself changes as NIFTY moves. Largest for ATM options on expiry day, when a small move flips an option from worthless to valuable.

Option chain

Option chain
NSE's table of every strike for one expiry: calls on the left, puts on the right, with price, IV, volume and open interest.
Open interest (OI)
How many contracts are open at that strike. Big OI at a strike means many sellers there; traders read a big CE OI as resistance and a big PE OI as support.
Change in OI
Contracts added or closed today. Rising OI with a rising price means new buying; rising OI with a falling price means new selling.
PCR
Put–call ratio: total PE OI divided by total CE OI. Above 1 means more put contracts are open.
Max pain
The strike at which option buyers, as a group, would lose the most on expiry. Some traders expect NIFTY to drift toward it.

Selling (writing)

Option seller
Collects the premium up front and keeps it if the option expires worthless. The most a seller can make is the premium; the loss can be far larger.
Margin
Cash the exchange blocks before you may sell an option, about ₹1.1–1.5 lakh per NIFTY lot (SPAN + exposure). A hedged spread blocks much less.
Mark to market (MTM)
Your open position's profit or loss at today's prices, settled daily into your account.
Square off
Close a position: sell what you bought, or buy back what you sold.

Costs

STT
Securities Transaction Tax: 0.15% of the premium when you sell an option, and 0.15% of the intrinsic value when a bought option is settled in the money (rates from April 2026). Nothing on a buy.
Other charges
Brokerage (₹20 per order at discount brokers), exchange transaction charge (~0.035% of premium), SEBI fee, stamp duty on buys, and 18% GST on brokerage and exchange charges. About ₹50–80 for a buy-and-sell on one lot.

Strategies

Spread
Buy one option and sell another of the same type at a different strike. A bull call spread (buy 24000 CE, sell 24100 CE) costs less than the call alone but caps the gain. A bear put spread is the mirror for a fall.
Credit spread
Sell an option and buy a further-out one as insurance. You collect a smaller premium, block far less margin, and your worst case is capped at the width minus the credit.
Straddle / strangle
Buy (or sell) both a CE and a PE. A long straddle bets on a big move either way; a short strangle bets NIFTY stays in a range and is a favourite, and a frequent disaster, on expiry day.
Hedge
A position that limits what another position can lose, like buying a PE to protect shares you own.
